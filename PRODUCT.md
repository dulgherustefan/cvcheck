# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Job seekers checking or improving a CV/resume, usually right before or after applying somewhere. Anxious about ATS rejection and recruiter first impressions. Free-tier users are casual and price-sensitive (one €1.99 one-time unlock is the conversion moment); Premium users are active job-hunters checking multiple roles. The homepage isn't just marketing that leads to a separate app: the actual analysis renders inline on the same page after submit, so the landing surface and the product experience are the same surface.

## Product Purpose

Free, instant, honest CV analysis: a score out of 100 across seven weighted dimensions, ATS compatibility, red flags ranked by severity, and a match score against a pasted job description. No signup required to see the real result. Success looks like: a visitor pastes/uploads a CV within seconds of landing, sees a credible score with a clear reason attached, and either fixes something themselves or pays €1.99 once for the full report.

## Brand Personality

Direct, honest, warm. The copy is blunt on purpose ("Brutally Honest", scores that don't flatter), but the visual system (cream background, terracotta accent) is meant to feel warmer and more human than a cold corporate SaaS dashboard. Confidence without corporate polish.

## Anti-references

Standard AI-generated design slop: gradient text, tiny uppercase tracked eyebrows above every section, the hero-metric template, identical card grids, side-stripe borders, glassmorphism-as-decoration, numbered section markers used as scaffolding rather than a real sequence.

## Design Principles

- Brutal honesty over flattery, in copy and in visuals. A low score should look and feel like a low score, not be softened.
- The free tier must feel like a complete, real result, not a teaser cut short. Nothing should look intentionally broken or blurred just to force payment; locked content should read as "more detail available," not "we hid the answer."
- Warm and human over corporate-SaaS-cold. Every design decision should ask whether it feels like software that's rooting for the user, not judging them from a distance.
- Clarity over decoration. A score, a red flag, or an ATS verdict must be scannable in under a second; motion and visual flourish support that legibility, they never compete with it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG target set; treat WCAG AA as the working floor (4.5:1 body text contrast, visible focus states). Reduced-motion users must get an equivalent static or crossfade experience for every animation, this product's score reveals and transitions are decorative, not load-bearing for comprehension.
